# PASS 1 — Structural diagnosis (whole manuscript)

## A. Argument

- **Central research question:** Why do algorithmically “optimal” circular pathways for Indian end-of-life electronics, especially smartphones, fail to become physical outcomes?
- **Main thesis:** Inferential capacity has decoupled from executional capacity. An AI recommendation is not circularity. The decoupling is named the **AI–Physical Execution Gap (AIPEG)**.
- **Theoretical contribution:** Executability as a conjunctive construct with five named conditions (**availability, accessibility, capacity, viability, legibility**); the Execution Feasibility Index \(\varphi\); the Circularity Execution Ratio (CER); **phantom circularity**; **Infrastructure-Constrained Circular AI (ICCAI)**.
- **Empirical / illustrative claims:** Facility counts, generation figures, official vs international denominators, informal-sector shares, and an illustrative reverse-logistics computation (Tables 3, B1, B2) showing consolidation dominating haulage by more than two orders of magnitude, and \(\varphi = 0\) wherever \(A(r) = 0\).
- **Causal / explanatory claims:** Spatial fungibility of EPR certificates rewards clustering; mass-based certificates do not pull activity up the R-hierarchy; informal channels outperform formal ones on accessibility, viability, and elastic capacity, and fail on legibility and environmental quality. These are institutional-mechanism arguments, not identified causal estimates.
- **Definitions:** AIPEG, phantom circularity, executability (five conditions), \(\varphi\), CER, ICCAI. Preserved verbatim in meaning.
- **Assumptions:** Logistic accessibility; conjunctive (multiplicative) \(\varphi\); illustrative freight and secondary-market parameters; binary \(A\) as a permissive upper bound.
- **Limitations (author’s own, preserved):** Unvalidated indices; illustrative not empirical computation; smartphone-specific economics.

## B. Academic structure

Introduction (vignette + theory of change + RQs) → conceptual foundations (R-hierarchy + AI families + executability) → formal definition and measurement → Indian material substrate → eight-stage bottleneck table → urban–rural break-even → EPR/DPI institutional analysis → ICCAI architecture → implications, limitations, SDGs → conclusion → declarations, references, Appendices A–B.

## C. Terminology retained

AIPEG; availability; accessibility; capacity; viability; legibility; Execution Feasibility Index \(\varphi\); Circularity Execution Ratio (CER); phantom circularity; ICCAI; R-hierarchy; EPR; CPCB; substrate-coupled / substrate-independent / substrate-conditional.

## D. Writing characteristics in the source overlay

Highly parallel taxonomies (four reasons, five conditions, four families, four properties, five principles); repeated “This is not X. It is Y.” antitheses; polished three-part sentences; uniform paragraph architecture; stacked abstract clauses; generic circular-economy literature openings. These, more than copied source prose, are what the AI-writing report is likely responding to. Similarity is already low (7%).
